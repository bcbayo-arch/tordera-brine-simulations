Brine plume of the Tordera desalination outfall - numerical simulations

High-resolution MITgcm simulations of the brine discharge, in two complementary configurations: a realistic nested model of the outfall area (~75 m horizontal resolution, 107 vertical levels, real bathymetry, boundary currents from the regional CATSEA model and hourly ERA5 wind, reference year 2022) and an idealised slope model that isolates the role of the wind. Every experiment compares two twin simulations, with and without the discharge, so what is shown is exactly the effect of the brine (S = 60 psu, 24.4 hm³/yr, diffuser at 21.5 m; the brine enters at the temperature of the receiving water, so the anomaly is purely haline).

ICM-CSIC · Elisa Berdalet, Bru Cabús, Xavier Garcia, Savitri Galiana Blanch, Joaquim Ballabrera Poy · August 2026

The four simulated windows

Window (2022)Discharge
1-15 April8-15 April
16-30 April23-30 April
1-15 July8-14 July
16-30 July23-30 July

In short: the brine always sinks from the diffuser and travels along the seabed. In April it drains down the slope to 90-140 m and dilutes quickly; in July it stays confined between 20 and 30 m close to the coast, the least dispersive case. The anomaly descends further in April than in July because in July a thermocline slows the descent of the brine plume and confines it to the upper layer.

The plume in 3D (real bathymetry)

Three-dimensional surfaces of the salinity excess over the real seabed of the outfall area. Each video covers the days of discharge, hour by hour. The star is the diffuser, the red line is the coastline. All four months share ONE colour scale (surfaces at 0.0092, 0.052, 0.30 and 1.71 psu, bar top 3.42 psu = the strongest week), so a surface that shrinks between months is a real change in the plume, not a change of scale.

Idealised slope: the plume in 3D

The same discharge on an idealised straight shelf slope, which isolates the effect of the wind from the complexity of the real bathymetry. Same construction as above: salinity-excess surfaces over the seabed, star = diffuser, red = shoreline. Both wind regimes share ONE colour scale (surfaces at 0.0092, 0.040, 0.18 and 0.77 psu), so the difference you see between them is the wind acting on the plume.
